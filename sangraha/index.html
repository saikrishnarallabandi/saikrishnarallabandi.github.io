Claims can change as the news develops

Sangraha revisits narratives as evidence changes. A claim can be revised, split into more specific claims, or retired. You can inspect its latest recorded assessment alongside the stories it includes.

What do Active, Dormant, Retired, and Pending mean?

Active

A narrative in the active collection. This does not mean its claim has been proven.

Dormant

No fresh evidence for 90 days. A quiet claim is not necessarily wrong.

Retired

Removed from the active collection after review or 180 days of inactivity. Its history remains available.

Pending

A review is unresolved. This describes the assessment, separately from whether the narrative is active or quiet.

How does Sangraha put this together?

Sources are collected, enriched, and grouped into article clusters. Generated story accounts retain links to their reporting. Narrative formation proposes connections across stories; evidence assessment checks and repairs those claims in bounded batches.

The architecture uses shared identities and typed relationships, inspired by Festival’s heterogeneous relation graphs. Keeping a story in a group is different from establishing that it supports the group’s claim.

The system checks that an assessment still refers to the current evidence before applying it. Exact quotations confirm where words appeared, but do not prove the interpretation. Automatic revival of dormant narratives remains unfinished.
